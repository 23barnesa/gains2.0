# GainLog hypertrophy evidence rules

GainLog uses research as programming guardrails, not as a claim that one routine or exercise is universally optimal. The user's schedule, recovery, adherence, equipment, performance history, pain, scoliosis constraints, and preferences still determine the recommendation.

## Rules encoded in the app and Coach

- **Split:** Split and full-body routines produce similar strength and hypertrophy outcomes when weekly volume is equated. GainLog chooses among PPL, full body, upper/lower, and the hybrid by schedule spacing, session length, recovery, and completion likelihood.
- **Frequency:** When volume is equated, frequency does not appear to meaningfully change hypertrophy. GainLog uses frequency to distribute weekly work and maintain set quality rather than maximizing frequency for its own sake.
- **Loading:** Hypertrophy can occur across a broad loading spectrum when sets have sufficient effort. GainLog keeps practical exercise-specific rep ranges and uses double progression.
- **Failure and RIR:** Momentary muscular failure is not required for hypertrophy. GainLog generally targets 1–2 RIR on compounds and 0–2 RIR on isolations, while protecting technique and joint safety.
- **Rest:** Current evidence suggests a small average hypertrophy benefit to rest intervals longer than 60 seconds, likely because they preserve training volume. GainLog's 60/75/90-second timers are starting points, not caps, and adapt upward when repeated comparable performance supports it.
- **Exercise selection:** Evidence does not establish a universally best exercise for every person. GainLog selects movements by target muscle, a comfortable controllable range of motion, stability, progression potential, available equipment, and user feedback.

## Key reviews

- Ramos-Campo et al. (2024), *Efficacy of Split Versus Full-Body Resistance Training on Strength and Muscle Growth: A Systematic Review With Meta-Analysis*, PMID [38595233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/).
- Schoenfeld et al. (2019), *How many times per week should a muscle be trained to maximize muscle hypertrophy? A systematic review and meta-analysis*, PMID [30558493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/).
- Lopez et al. (2021), *Resistance Training Load Effects on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gain: Systematic Review and Network Meta-analysis*, PMID [33433148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33433148/).
- Refalo et al. (2023), *Influence of Resistance Training Proximity-to-Failure on Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis*, PMID [36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/).
- Singer et al. (2024), *Give it a rest: a systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis on the effect of inter-set rest interval duration on muscle hypertrophy*, PMID [39205815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/).

These rules should be revisited when strong new syntheses materially change the evidence base.
